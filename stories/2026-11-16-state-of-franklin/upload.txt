TITLE:
America almost had a 14th state called Franklin

DESCRIPTION:
In 1784, North Carolina offered its lands west of the Appalachians to Congress. Settlers in what is now East Tennessee met at Jonesborough, declared their own state and named it Franklin, after Benjamin Franklin, hoping to become the 14th state. North Carolina repealed the cession and reclaimed the land, and Franklin's bid for statehood fell short of the nine states needed under the Articles of Confederation. Its governor, John Sevier, was arrested for treason in 1788 but never tried; in 1796 he became the first governor of Tennessee, the 16th state.
