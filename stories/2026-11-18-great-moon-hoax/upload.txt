TITLE:
A newspaper convinced readers the moon had bat people

DESCRIPTION:
On August 25, 1835, The Sun of New York began a series of six articles claiming that astronomer Sir John Herschel had observed life on the moon through a giant telescope with a seven-ton lens: unicorns, two-legged beavers and furry winged humans known as man-bats. The real Herschel was working at the Cape of Good Hope in South Africa and had nothing to do with the story. The paper's sales shot up, and on September 16, 1835, The Sun admitted the articles were fabricated. They are generally attributed to reporter Richard Adams Locke.
