TITLE:
80 million trees fell, and there was no crater

DESCRIPTION:
On the morning of June 30, 1908, a fireball streaked over a remote part of Siberia and exploded in the air near the Tunguska River. The space rock, most likely an asteroid, blew apart about 3 to 6 miles (5 to 10 km) above the ground, flattening an estimated 80 million trees across about 830 square miles (2,150 sq km) of forest, an area bigger than New York City. About 40 miles from the blast, an eyewitness felt a wave of heat "as if my shirt had caught fire." For nights afterward the skies over Europe and Asia glowed, and Londoners could read newsprint at midnight without lamps. The first scientific expedition, led by Leonid Kulik, did not reach the site until 1927; Kulik went looking for a meteorite crater, but there was none.
