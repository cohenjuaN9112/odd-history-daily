TITLE:
America and Britain nearly went to war over a pig

DESCRIPTION:
The 1846 Oregon Treaty ran the U.S.-British border through the middle of the channel separating the continent from Vancouver Island, but there were two channels, Haro Strait and Rosario Strait, and both nations claimed the San Juan Islands between them. On June 15, 1859, American settler Lyman Cutlar shot a Hudson's Bay Company pig that was rooting for potatoes in his garden on San Juan Island. By the end of August, 461 U.S. soldiers with 14 cannons faced British warships, but Rear Admiral Robert L. Baynes refused to start a war over a pig. The two sides shared the island under joint military occupation for 12 years, until an arbitration under Germany's Kaiser Wilhelm I gave the islands to the United States in October 1872, and the only casualty of the Pig War was the pig.
