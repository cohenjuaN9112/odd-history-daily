TITLE:
The day noon happened twice in America

DESCRIPTION:
Before standard time, every American town kept its own local time by the sun, and the country ran on more than 100 local times: when it was noon in Chicago, it was about 11:27 in Omaha and 11:50 in St. Louis. On Sunday, November 18, 1883, the railroads switched to standard time, splitting the U.S. into four zones based on the 75th, 90th, 105th and 120th meridians, each one hour apart. Clocks were reset at noon, so several cities heard noon twice: in New York, church bells rang noon by the old local time and again about four minutes later by the new standard time. Congress did not make standard time law until the Standard Time Act of March 19, 1918, 35 years later.
