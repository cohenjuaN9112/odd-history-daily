TITLE:
A chimp beat every American astronaut to space

DESCRIPTION:
On January 31, 1961, NASA launched a chimpanzee named Ham on the Mercury-Redstone 2 test flight from Cape Canaveral, more than three months before Alan Shepard became the first American in space. A problem with the Redstone rocket made it burn too hard, so the capsule climbed to about 157 miles instead of the planned 115 and splashed down more than 120 miles past its target; Ham was recovered alive and was given an apple. Because of the thrust problem, NASA's Marshall team added an extra unmanned booster test flight (MR-BD) on March 24, 1961, and on April 12 the Soviet Union's Yuri Gagarin became the first human in space. Shepard flew on May 5, 1961.

Sources: NASA, "Mercury-Redstone Launch Vehicle" (Marshall Space Flight Center history); AmericaSpace, "'We Gave It Away': Remembering the Unhappy Flight of Ham the Chimp, 60 Years On" (2021); Wikipedia, "Mercury-Redstone 2"; NASA Image and Video Library captions for MR-2 (S63-22731) and MR-3 (S61-01928).
Images: NASA, public domain: Ham before the flight and being fitted into his couch; the MR-2 launch; Ham's recovery aboard the USS Donner; Ham after his recovery; Earth seen from the MR-2 capsule (Johnson Space Center); the MR-BD test launch of March 24, 1961, and Alan Shepard with the Freedom 7 spacecraft (Marshall Space Flight Center).
