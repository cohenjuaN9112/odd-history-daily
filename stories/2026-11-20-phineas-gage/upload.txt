TITLE:
An iron rod shot through his skull, and he lived

DESCRIPTION:
On September 13, 1848, near Cavendish, Vermont, railroad construction foreman Phineas Gage was preparing a blast when his tamping iron struck a spark and set off the powder. The 13-pound iron, 3 feet 7 inches long, went in under his left cheekbone, came out through the top of his head and landed about 80 feet away, yet he was conscious and able to speak moments later. His personality changed so much that his friends said he was "no longer Gage"; he later drove stagecoaches in Chile for about seven years and died in California in 1860, nearly 12 years after the accident. His skull and the tamping iron are on display at Harvard Medical School's Warren Anatomical Museum.
