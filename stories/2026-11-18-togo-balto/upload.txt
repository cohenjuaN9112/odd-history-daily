TITLE:
America gave the statue to the wrong dog

DESCRIPTION:
In early 1925, a diphtheria outbreak in Nome, Alaska, set off a relay of 20 mushers and about 150 sled dogs that carried antitoxin serum 674 miles from Nenana in about five days (127.5 hours). Balto led Gunnar Kaasen's team on the final 53-mile leg, became a national celebrity, and had a statue in New York's Central Park by December 1925. Leonhard Seppala's team, led by Togo, ran about 261 miles, including the most dangerous stretch of the route. A bronze statue of Togo, sculpted in 2001, stands in Seward Park in New York City and received its plaque only in 2020. The archival photos show Alaska dog teams of the era, not the 1925 relay itself.
