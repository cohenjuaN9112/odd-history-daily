TITLE:
Why Canada sends Boston a Christmas tree every year

DESCRIPTION:
On December 6, 1917, the French munitions ship Mont-Blanc collided with the Norwegian ship Imo in Halifax Harbour, Nova Scotia, and exploded in what is described as the largest man-made explosion before the atomic bomb, killing nearly 2,000 people. That night, Boston sent a relief train with medical help and supplies; a blizzard delayed it, and it reached Halifax on the morning of December 8. In 1918, Nova Scotia sent Boston a Christmas tree as a thank-you, and since 1971 it has sent one every year to be lit on Boston Common. This year's tree, a 46-foot white spruce from East Amherst, Nova Scotia, was cut on November 18, 2026.
