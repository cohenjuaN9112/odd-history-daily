TITLE:
Chicago blamed its Great Fire on a cow. It was framed

DESCRIPTION:
The Great Chicago Fire broke out on the night of October 8, 1871, in or near the barn of Patrick and Catherine O'Leary on DeKoven Street; it killed about 300 people and left some 100,000 homeless, about a third of the city. Newspapers spread the story that Mrs. O'Leary's cow had kicked over a lantern, although she testified that she was in bed and the official inquiry could not determine the cause. Michael Ahern, a reporter for the Chicago Republican, later claimed he had made up the cow story, and in 1997 the Chicago City Council passed a resolution exonerating Catherine O'Leary and her cow. Since 1961, the Chicago Fire Academy has stood on the old O'Leary property.
