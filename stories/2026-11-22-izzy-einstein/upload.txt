TITLE:
Prohibition's top agent was a 225-pound postal clerk

DESCRIPTION:
Isidor "Izzy" Einstein, a 5-foot-5, 225-pound New York postal clerk, became a federal Prohibition agent in 1920. Working with his partner Moe Smith, a former cigar store owner, he caught bootleggers in dozens of disguises, from gravedigger and fisherman to football player and trombone player, and saved the drinks he was served as evidence with a funnel sewn into his vest that ran to a hidden container; in New Orleans, it took him just 35 seconds to be offered a drink. The pair made 4,932 arrests, with a 95 percent conviction rate. In November 1925 they were let go, officially for not measuring up to "standards of efficiency," though their superiors were said to resent all the headlines they made.
