TITLE:
An Olympic marathon was won with rat poison

DESCRIPTION:
At the 1904 Olympics in St. Louis, the marathon was run on August 30 in heat above 90 degrees, on dusty roads, with only one official water stop. Fred Lorz crossed the finish line first, but he had ridden about 11 miles in a car after cramping at mile 9, and he was banned for life by the AAU. The gold went to Thomas Hicks, whose handlers kept him going with strychnine (today best known as a rat poison, then used in small doses as a stimulant), egg whites and brandy; he had to be held up to cross the line. Only 14 of the 32 starters finished.
